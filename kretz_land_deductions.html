The five rules.

How appraisers and careful buyers keep deductions honest. The evaluator enforces all five.

Start unencumbered

Begin from the value of similar land without the problem, taken from comparable sales. Write down where that number came from.

Map the acres

Measure the affected area from a map, survey or recorded document. A strip easement is width × length ÷ 43,560.

Deduct where it hits

Apply the discount to the affected acres only, unless the condition truly affects the whole parcel (no access, conservation easement).

Count every acre once

A wetland inside a powerline corridor is one deduction, under the more severe of the two. Soils already priced through timber value are not deducted again.

Prove the percentage

Support each percentage with paired sales or a documented market study. Otherwise label it as judgment and get sign-off.

    Prompts for deduction work.
    Use AI to read documents and organize evidence. Not to invent the percentage.
    

    

    📄
    Read an easement document
    Prompt
    Here is the text of a recorded easement: [paste].
List: who holds it; its width and location; what the holder may do; what the landowner may still do; whether trees may grow in it; whether it can be relocated; whether it runs with the land.
Quote the clause that answers each item. If the document does not say, write "Not stated". Do not estimate any value.
    

    ⚖️
    Paired sales for one condition
    Prompt
    Here are sales of similar parcels: [paste table: date, acres, price, location, has the condition yes/no].
Pair sales that differ mainly by [condition]. For each pair, show the price-per-acre difference as a percentage, with the arithmetic.
Then give the range and median across pairs. Flag pairs that also differ in size, date, access or location.
Use only the sales I gave you. If there are too few pairs to support a percentage, say so.
    

    🔁
    Check for double counting
    Prompt
    Here are the deductions applied to a parcel: [paste the evaluator summary].
Identify anything that could be counted twice: overlapping areas (e.g. wetland inside an easement), soil or productivity limits already reflected in timber value, or conditions my comparable sales already share.
Do not change any percentage. List the questions I need to answer instead.
